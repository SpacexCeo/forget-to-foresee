# How much time a judge gives one project (all criteria)

Research cycle 8, 2026-11-08 23:50 UTC. Search-result snippets read. Moderate depth.

## Findings
- MLH plans about 4 minutes per project in expo judging (2 min demo, 1 min questions and scoring, 1 min travel) and
  a 2 to 3 hour window overall: https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/judging-and-submissions/judging-plan
  (2026-11-08).
- Online and hybrid events use asynchronous review of 2 to 5 minute demo videos, scored on the judge's own time; no
  per-video figure: https://medium.com/techtogether/how-to-organize-judging-for-your-virtual-hackathon-43ce715f95dd
  (2026-11-08).
- A 2018 organizer report: submissions usually judged in under 5 minutes: https://dod.ngo/blog/2018/our-judging-process
  (2026-11-08).
- A planning guide budgets 90 minutes for 50 projects including onboarding, about 1.5 minutes each:
  https://intercom.help/builderbase/en/articles/14831464-hackathon-playbook-ch-15-judging-rubrics (2026-11-08;
  a plan, not a measurement).
- No measured figure exists for asynchronous online judging with hundreds of entries.

## Consequence for the rubric
The NEAR jury faces about 311 entries (directory, 2026-11-08) and seven investor jurors after an AI screener. A human
juror plausibly gives a project 3 to 5 minutes before deciding whether to look deeper. The procedure therefore runs a
**five-minute first pass** before the full checklists: what can be seen in 5 minutes from the X post, the README top
and the live link (no clone) is scored as "first impression" and reported beside the full score. A large gap between
the two (full score more than 15 points above the first-pass estimate) is reported as a presentation risk, because
most jurors will only ever see the first pass.
